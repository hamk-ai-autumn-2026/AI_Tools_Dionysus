Everest Base Camp Trek

FLAGSHIP ROUTE TEA-HOUSE TREK Guide updated 3 days ago · Reviewed by the HimalRoute editor team

The Everest Base Camp (EBC) trek is the classic Khumbu Valley journey to the foot of the world's highest mountain. Starting with the famous mountain landing at Lukla, the trail climbs through Sherpa villages, pine forest and high alpine valleys to Everest Base Camp at 5,364 m, with most itineraries adding the sunrise viewpoint of Kala Patthar (5,545 m) for the best close-up panorama of Everest, Nuptse and the Khumbu Glacier.

The route follows the Dudh Koshi river through Phakding to Namche Bazaar (3,440 m), the Sherpa "capital" of Khumbu, where almost every itinerary includes an acclimatisation day. Beyond Namche the trail passes the monastery town of Tengboche, the yak pastures of Dingboche and the memorial chortens above Lobuche before reaching Gorak Shep, the last settlement, from which both Base Camp and Kala Patthar are walked as day trips.

Permits you need

Trekkers in the Khumbu need a Sagarmatha National Park entry permit, the Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality fee (collected at Lukla or Monjo), and a TIMS (Trekkers' Information Management System) card. There is no separate "Everest permit"; costs are paid in Nepali rupees at official counters in Kathmandu or at the trailhead checkpoints, and passports are stamped along the way.

Altitude and safety

The single most important rule of EBC is to gain altitude slowly: the itinerary above builds in two acclimatisation days precisely because rapid ascent is the main cause of Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS). Drink 3–4 litres of water daily, ascend no more than ~500 m of sleeping altitude per day above 3,000 m, and never ignore symptoms such as persistent headache, nausea or unusual fatigue. Travel insurance covering high-altitude helicopter evacuation is strongly recommended.
