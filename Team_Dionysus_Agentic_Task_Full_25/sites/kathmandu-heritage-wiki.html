Pashupatinath Temple

UNESCO WORLD HERITAGE — KATHMANDU VALLEY (INSCRIBED 1979)PAGODA STYLE   Last reviewed 4 days ago by EditorPratima
PASHUPATINATH
Editor's drawing: the two-tiered golden roof above the western gate
DeityShiva as Pashupatinath ("Lord of Animals")
LocationBank of the Bagmati River, Deopatan, Kathmandu
StyleNepali pagoda, two-tiered golden roof
UNESCO statusInscribed 1979 (Kathmandu Valley group)
Managed byPashupatinath Area Development Trust
Major festivalMaha Shivaratri (Feb–Mar)

Pashupatinath Temple is the most important Hindu shrine in Nepal and one of the four most sacred temples of Lord Shiva on the subcontinent. Standing on the eastern bank of the holy Bagmati River at Deopatan, just east of central Kathmandu, the temple complex spreads over a walled precinct of shrines, ghats, ashrams and groves, and it draws pilgrims, sadhus and mourners every single day of the year. Non-Hindu visitors may view the main temple from the eastern bank across the river, where a full panorama of the pagoda unfolds above the cremation terraces.

The sanctum houses the lingam of Pashupatinath — Shiva worshipped here as the shepherd of all living beings. Only ordained priests enter the inner sanctum, and the four carved silver doors open for specific daily rituals whose bells echo across the riverbank from before dawn until evening.

History

Ancient era
A shrine to Shiva on the Bagmati is believed to predate the written records of the valley; legends attribute its origin to a miraculously self-formed lingam discovered by a cowherd's cow.
Medieval period
Successive royal dynasties of the Kathmandu Valley rebuilt and expanded the complex; the present two-tiered pagoda form reflects later Newari craftsmanship.
1979
Pashupatinath is inscribed — together with the valley's durbar squares, stupas and Changu Narayan — as part of the Kathmandu Valley World Heritage Site.
Today
A freely accessible riverside precinct managed by the Pashupatinath Area Development Trust, combining living worship with heritage conservation.

Architecture

The main temple is a cubical sanctum crowned by a two-tiered roof gilded in gold, rising above four silver-plated doors and gilded doorways carved with guardian figures. Around the compound stand hundreds of smaller shrines, including the rows of Shiva lingams with yoni plinths along the western walkway, the Vasuki Nath and Unmatt Bhairav shrines, and the terraced Arya Ghat where cremation fires burn beside the river. The Bagmati's stone embankments and the nine-arched footbridge upstream form the classic viewing composition that every visitor photographs.

Rituals and festivals

Worship follows a fixed daily rhythm of rituals at dawn, midday and dusk, punctuated by the ringing of the great bell. The riverside aarati at Arya Ghat each evening — rows of flame lamps, conch calls and drums facing the water — has become one of Kathmandu's most moving spectacles. On Maha Shivaratri, tens of thousands of devotees and ash-smeared sadhus fill the complex through the night, and during Teej the courtyards fill with women in red celebrating together.

"Pashupatinath is where the valley takes both its prayers and its farewells. Life, death and devotion share the same flight of stone steps here."
